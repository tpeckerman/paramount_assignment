## Article 1 — General violence limit (as replaced by 2014-17)

**Between 06:00 and 22:00**, and *a fortiori* in programmes intended for young audiences, violence —
**including psychological violence** — must not be able to be perceived as continuous, as the only
solution to conflicts, or in an exacerbated form.

- Programmes on **historical or controversial** subjects may be broadcast before 22:00 **if** the editor
  provides educational support going beyond a mere written or spoken warning.
- For **difficult images or testimony about particularly dramatic events** in news and other programmes,
  the editor must take precautions and **warn the public in advance**.

> **Note:** this is a **standalone time-based content rule that is independent of the category system.**
> A programme can be category I / II and still breach Article 1. A solution that only reasons about
> categories will miss it.

## Article 2 — The five categories (*signalétique jeunesse*)

The editor uses a **commission de visionnage** (internal viewing committee) which recommends a classification.
The editor applies one of five degrees and the corresponding on-screen signage.

| Category | Pictogram | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | none | Programmes for all audiences (*tous publics*). |
| **II** | white circle, black **-10** | Programmes **certain scenes of which are liable to shock** younger viewers / minors under 10. |
| **III** | white circle, black **-12** | Cinematographic works **banned to under-12s in cinemas**, and programmes **liable to disturb** under-12s, **in particular where the scenario resorts in a repeated and systematic way to physical or psychological violence**. |
| **IV** | white circle, black **-16** | Cinematographic works banned to under-16s in cinemas, and programmes **of an erotic character or of great violence**, liable to harm the physical, mental or moral development of under-16s. |
| **V** | white circle, black **-18** | Cinematographic works banned to under-18s in cinemas, and **pornographic programmes or programmes of very great violence**, reserved for a **warned adult audience** and liable to harm the physical, mental or moral development of under-18s. |

### Relationship to the cinema classification (the film carry-over rule)
- The classification a film receives for **cinema release may serve as an indication** for its TV classification.
- **But** the editor must check that the rating transfers without harm and **may strengthen it.**
  French practice is explicitly to **raise the age limit** between cinema and television, because a child who
  could not get into the cinema can easily be in front of a TV set.
- **Reclassification procedure for older films:** works **at least 20 years** since their last visa may be
  submitted for revision for TV broadcast, by **joint request** of the editor and the producer (or authorised
  agent), at least **8 months** before scheduling. Arcom forwards up to **20 requests per year** to the
  Commission de classification des œuvres cinématographiques, which must give its opinion at least
  **1 month** before broadcast.

> **This is the mechanism that makes "age rating ≠ broadcast hours" concrete in France too:**
> the TV category is a *separate determination* from the cinema visa, and may be stricter.

## Article 3 — Programming conditions (as replaced by 2014-17)

**This is the operative scheduling table for France.**

| Category | Cinema channels & pay-per-view services | Other television services (incl. free-to-air) |
|---|---|---|
| **I** (tous publics) | No restriction | No restriction |
| **II** (-10) | Timing at the **editor's discretion**, but **may not be placed within programmes intended for children**. Particular care for trailers near children's programmes. | Same |
| **III** (-12) | **Must not be broadcast on Wednesdays before 20:30** | **Must not be broadcast before 22:00** |
| **III** — exceptional derogation (all services) | May exceptionally be broadcast **after 20:30**, **except** on **Tuesdays, Fridays, Saturdays, the eve of public holidays, and during school holidays**. Capped at **16 programmes per year**, of which at most **4** may be works banned to under-12s in cinemas. Remains subject to Article 1 ¶1. | Same |
| **IV** (-16) | Reserved for a warned audience. **After 20:30** | Reserved for a warned audience. **After 22:30** |
| **V** (-18) | Governed **entirely** by **Recommandation n° 2004-7 du 15 décembre 2004** | Same (see FR_03) |

### Absolute prohibitions (no time or service unlocks these)
Programmes which **offend human dignity** — in particular those depicting **sexual violence, perversions or
degradation** — are **banned from broadcast**. The same applies to **pornographic programmes depicting
under-18s** and to programmes of **extreme or gratuitous violence**.

## Article 4 — On-screen signage (as replaced by 2014-17, art. 4(2)(a) by 2012-57)

- The classification must be made known to the public **at the time of broadcast**, in **trailers**, and in
  **programme schedules communicated to the press**.
- The **pictogram must remain on screen for the whole duration** of the programme (categories II–V under the
  2014 text; under the original 2005 text category II was 5 minutes at the start and III–V throughout).
- A **written mention** must also appear, either **at the bottom of the screen in white for at least
  1 minute** at the start, **or full-screen before the programme for at least 12 seconds**:
  - **II** — "déconseillé aux moins de dix ans"
  - **III** — "déconseillé aux moins de douze ans", or the under-18 cinema ban wording where assigned
  - **IV** — "déconseillé aux moins de seize ans", or the assigned ban wording
  - **V** — "déconseillé aux moins de dix-huit ans"
- Décret n° 90-174 on prior public warnings continues to apply.

> **Note for the rule table:** signage is a **separate obligation from scheduling**. A programme can be
> correctly scheduled and still breach by failing to display the pictogram. The C8 enforcement decision
> (FR-V1) was precisely about signage obligations, citing art. 4.

## Article 5 — Other programmes: trailers and music videos (inserted by 2014-17)

### Trailers (*bandes-annonces*)
- Must display the **pictogram of the programme's category throughout** the trailer.
- Must **not contain scenes likely to offend** young viewers.
- **Category II** trailers shown near children's programmes require particular care.
- **Category III** trailers **may not air close to children's programmes**.
- **Category IV** trailers **may not air before 20:30**.

### Music videos (*vidéomusiques*)
- **Exempt from systematic signage.**
- Programmes containing music videos whose **themes are not aimed at children or teenagers** should
  nonetheless carry a **warning**.
- Music videos **likely to offend young viewers should preferably be broadcast after 22:00.**

> **Important for test-case design:** France treats music videos as a **special regime** —
> signage-exempt, with a **"preferably after 22:00"** soft rule. The UK (UK-G1) treats them under the ordinary
> watershed with detailed content thresholds. **The same music video can therefore produce a hard UK verdict
> and a soft French one.** This asymmetry is a strong test case and a good illustration of why the two
> countries cannot share a single decision path.

## Design implications

1. **France's category and its hours are linked but not identical**, and the link is
   **service-type dependent** (cinema/PPV vs other) and **day-of-week dependent** (Wednesday rule,
   Tuesday/Friday/Saturday/holiday-eve/school-holiday exclusions). The output therefore needs a
   **broadcast date/day and channel type**, not just a time. Another missing-information trigger.
2. **The derogation is quota-limited** (16/year, max 4 cinema-banned-to-under-12s). A per-programme prompt
   **cannot** verify a quota — it has no view of the channel's annual schedule. The correct behaviour is to
   state the derogation exists and **flag that quota verification requires channel-level data / human review.**
3. **Article 1 is category-independent.** Needs its own rule row.
4. **Signage is a distinct output.** For France the deliverable should arguably be three things:
   category, permitted hours, **and** signage obligation.
