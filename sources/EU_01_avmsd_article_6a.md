# EU-D1 — AVMSD Article 6a (Directive 2010/13/EU as amended by Directive (EU) 2018/1808)

- **Body:** European Union (Parliament and Council); supervised nationally by each Member State's regulator,
  coordinated through **ERGA** (European Regulators Group for Audiovisual Media Services)
- **Source type:** statute (authority rank 1)
- **URL (consolidated, as retained in UK law):** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2010/13/article/6a
- **Plain view:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2010/13/article/6a/data.htm?view=plain
- **Observatory overview:** https://www.obs.coe.int/en/web/observatoire/protection-of-minors
- **Retrieval status:** STRUCTURED EXTRACT (4 paragraphs).

> **Applicability.** The AVMSD is **not directly applicable** to a broadcast — it binds Member States, who
> implement it nationally. It is included here because it is the **common ancestor** of both national
> frameworks and explains their structure. It should sit in the rule table as `applies_directly = no`,
> `role = framework_baseline`, so that it is never cited as the operative rule for a decision.

## Article 6a(1) — Protection of minors from harmful content

Member States must take appropriate measures to ensure that audiovisual media services provided by media
service providers under their jurisdiction **which may impair the physical, mental or moral development of
minors** are only made available **in such a way as to ensure that minors will not normally hear or see them**.

- Such measures **may include** selecting the **time of broadcast**, **age verification tools**, or
  **other technical measures**.
- They must be **proportionate to the potential harm** of the programme.
- **"The most harmful content, such as gratuitous violence and pornography, shall be subject to the
  strictest measures."**

## Article 6a(2) — Minors' personal data

Personal data of minors collected or generated by media service providers pursuant to paragraph 1 must
**not be processed for commercial purposes**, such as direct marketing, profiling or behaviourally targeted
advertising.

## Article 6a(3) — Content information / descriptors

Member States must ensure that media service providers **provide sufficient information to viewers about
content which may impair** minors' physical, mental or moral development. For this purpose, providers shall
use **a system describing the potentially harmful nature of the content**. Member States are encouraged to
use **co-regulation** as provided for in Article 4a(1).

## Article 6a(4) — Best practice and self-regulation

The Commission is to encourage providers to exchange best practice on co-regulatory codes of conduct;
Member States and the Commission may promote self-regulation through Union codes of conduct per Article 4a(2).

## Related provisions
- **Article 28b(1)(a)** — equivalent duty for **video-sharing platforms**, with "the most harmful content"
  subject to the **strictest access control measures**. (Out of scope: not linear TV.)
- **Article 8** — Member States must ensure providers do not transmit cinematographic works outside periods
  agreed with rights holders. (Contractual windowing, not a minors rule — noted to avoid confusion.)

## UK position post-Brexit
The UK is no longer bound by the directive, but **The Audiovisual Media Services Regulations 2020
(SI 2020/1062)** implemented the revised duties into UK law. Its explanatory note confirms that audiovisual
media services must protect minors **"using measures proportionate to the potential harm, including through
selecting the time of the broadcast, age verification tools or other technical measures"**, and that the
detailed content standards for broadcast television remain in **Ofcom's Broadcasting Code**.
→ So the UK's substantive position is materially aligned, but the **operative source is always the Ofcom Code**.

## Design implications — why the EU layer justifies the whole architecture

1. **Article 6a mandates an outcome, not a method.** "Minors will not normally hear or see them" is the
   target; broadcast time, age verification and other technical measures are listed as alternative means.
   This is the *root cause* of the assignment's warning not to assume a rating→hours mapping:
   **two Member States can lawfully reach the same outcome with completely different instruments.**
   - France → graded descriptors (I–V) + fixed hours per category per service type + locking for -18.
   - UK → single watershed + contextual judgement + PIN-based MDP, and **no per-programme age rating**.
2. **Proportionality is built in.** "Proportionate to the potential harm" means the *strength* of the content
   drives the *strictness* of the measure. This supports a design where the extraction stage records
   **intensity**, not merely category presence.
3. **Article 6a(3) explains why descriptors and age limits are separate things.** The directive requires an
   age/harm **descriptor system** *in addition to* access measures. France's 2021 transposition (Décret du
   4 février 2021) is noted in EU notification material as requiring **content indicators/descriptors
   (violence, sex, distress, etc.)** because the existing programme symbol gave no indication of the *type*
   of harmful content.
   → **This is independent corroboration for the design decision to carry a fixed `content_category`
   dimension (violence / sex / drugs / language / fear …) in the rule table, separate from the age level.**
   The regulators themselves concluded that an age number alone is insufficient.
4. **For Task 2 (scaling to dozens of countries):** Article 6a is the **shared spine**. Every EU/EEA country's
   framework is an implementation of the same three obligations, which means a cross-country schema can
   reasonably standardise on: *harm category → severity → permitted-availability mechanism (time window /
   access control / prohibition) → descriptor obligation*. The country-specific part is the *mapping*, not
   the concepts. Non-EU countries will not share this spine and should not be forced into it.
