## Principle

"To ensure that people under eighteen are protected."

## Key definitions

### "Children"
People **under fifteen**. Note the asymmetry: the *principle* protects under-eighteens, but the
operative rules that turn on the word "children" use **under 15**. This matters for rule application.

### "Appropriate scheduling" (Rule 1.3)
Judged according to:
- the nature of the content;
- the likely number and age range of children in the audience, **taking into account school time,
  weekends and holidays**;
- the start and finish time of the programme;
- the nature of the channel or programme;
- the likely expectations of the audience for that channel or station at that time and on that day.

Broadcasters serving ECTT Parties should also consider relevant time differences in the country of reception.

### The watershed
- **21:00 to 05:30.** Material unsuitable for children should not, in general, be shown before 21:00 or after 05:30.
- **Applies to television only** (radio uses "when children are particularly likely to be listening").
- **Premium subscription film services without protection:** watershed is **20:00**.
- **Services with mandatory daytime protection in place:** the watershed does not apply (see UK-M1).

## Rules

### General protection
- **1.1** Material that might seriously impair the physical, mental or moral development of people under
  eighteen must not be broadcast. *(Absolute prohibition — no contextual justification available.)*
- **1.2** Broadcasters must take all reasonable steps to protect people under eighteen in providing services.

### Scheduling and content information
- **1.3** Children must be protected by **appropriate scheduling** from material that is unsuitable for them.
  (BBC ODPS must provide equivalent protection.)
- **1.4** Television broadcasters **must observe the watershed** (21:00). *(Bright-line rule.)*
- **1.5** Radio: must have particular regard to times when children are particularly likely to be listening
  (e.g. the school run, breakfast). *(Not applicable to TV.)*
- **1.6** **Transition rule.** The transition to more adult material must not be unduly abrupt at the
  watershed. The strongest material should appear **later** in the schedule.
- **1.7** Clear information about content that may distress some children should be given, if appropriate,
  to the audience, taking into account the context.

### Coverage of sexual and other offences involving under-18s
- **1.8** Care must be taken not to include clues that could lead to the identification of under-18s
  involved in sexual offence cases.
- **1.9** Particular care before broadcasting identifying details of under-18s in pre-trial investigations.

### Drugs, smoking, solvents and alcohol
- **1.10** Must not be featured in programmes made primarily for children unless there is strong editorial
  justification; must not be condoned, encouraged or glamorised in other programmes broadcast before the
  watershed, or when children are particularly likely to be listening, unless there is editorial justification.

### Violence and dangerous behaviour
- **1.11** Violence, its after-effects and descriptions of violence, whether verbal or physical, must be
  appropriately limited in programmes broadcast before the watershed and must be justified by the context.
- **1.12** Must not be material that condones or glamorises violent, dangerous or seriously antisocial
  behaviour likely to encourage imitation, in programmes made primarily for children.
- **1.13** Dangerous behaviour, or behaviour easily imitable by children in a manner that is harmful,
  must not be featured before the watershed unless there is editorial justification. *(See also Rules 2.4, 2.5.)*

### Offensive language
- **1.14** **The most offensive language must not be broadcast before the watershed** (or when children are
  particularly likely to be listening). *(Bright-line rule — no contextual justification available pre-watershed.)*
- **1.15** Offensive language must not be broadcast in programmes made for younger children.
- **1.16** Offensive language must not be broadcast before the watershed unless justified by the context.
  In any event, **frequent use** of such language must be avoided before the watershed. *(See also Rule 2.3.)*

### Sexual material
- **1.17** Material equivalent to the BBFC **R18** rating must not be broadcast **at any time**.
  *(Absolute prohibition.)*
- **1.18** "Adult sex material" — material that contains images and/or language of a strong sexual nature
  broadcast for the primary purpose of sexual arousal or stimulation — must not be broadcast at any time
  **other than between 22:00 and 05:30** on premium subscription services and pay-per-view/night services
  which operate with **mandatory restricted access**. *(Bright-line window + access condition.)*
- **1.19** Broadcasters must ensure that material broadcast after the watershed which contains images and/or
  language of a strong or explicit sexual nature, but is not "adult sex material", is justified by the context.
- **1.20** Representations of sexual intercourse must not occur before the watershed unless there is a
  **serious educational purpose**. Any discussion on, or portrayal of, sexual behaviour must be editorially
  justified if included before the watershed and must be appropriately limited.
- **1.21** Nudity before the watershed must be justified by the context.

### Films, premium subscription film services, pay-per-view services
- **1.22** No film refused classification by the **BBFC** may be broadcast unless it has subsequently been
  classified, or the BBFC has confirmed it would not be subject to compulsory cuts under current standards.
  Restrictions also apply to cut versions.
- **1.23** **BBFC 18-rated films (or their equivalent) must not be broadcast before 21:00 on any service**,
  except where there is **mandatory daytime protection** in place. *(Bright-line rule, amended 2019.)*
- **1.24** Where there is **mandatory daytime protection** (i.e. a PIN protection system pre-2100 and
  post-0530 — or pre-2000 for films up to BBFC 15 on premium subscription film services), programmes
  unsuitable for children that would usually be scheduled after the watershed, and films up to BBFC 18 or
  equivalent, **may be broadcast at any time of day**. Clear information must be provided about the content
  and about the protection in place. Without such protection, the normal watershed rules apply.
- **1.25** **Pay-per-view** services may broadcast films up to BBFC 18 or equivalent **at any time**, provided
  mandatory daytime protection is in place. They must also provide content information, operate a detailed
  itemised billing system, and make subscribers aware of their child-protection systems.
- **1.26** BBFC **R18**-rated films must not be broadcast. *(Absolute prohibition.)*

### Exorcism, the occult and the paranormal
- **1.27** Demonstrations of exorcism, occult practices or paranormal activity **presented as real** must not
  be shown before the watershed. Programmes in which such activity is treated as entertainment must not be
  broadcast when significant numbers of children may be expected to be watching.
  **Drama, film and comedy are exempt.**

### Under-18s taking part in programmes
- **1.28** Due care must be taken over the physical and emotional welfare and the dignity of people under
  eighteen who take part or are otherwise involved in programmes, **irrespective of any consent** given by
  the participant or by a parent, guardian or other person over fifteen *in loco parentis*.
- **1.29** People under eighteen must not be caused unnecessary distress or anxiety by their involvement
  in programmes or by the broadcast of those programmes.
- **1.30** Prizes aimed at children must be appropriate to the age range of both the target audience and
  the participants.

## Service-scope notes (critical for the rule table)

- **Footnote 6 / Note:** the scheduling requirements in **Rules 1.3, 1.4, 1.5 and 1.6 do not apply to BBC
  on-demand programme services (BBC ODPS).**
- The watershed itself is a **television** concept. For radio the equivalent is the
  "children particularly likely to be listening" test.
- **Premium subscription film services** and **pay-per-view services** have distinct carve-outs
  (1.18, 1.23, 1.24, 1.25). A rule table that does not carry `service_type` will get these wrong.

## Design implications

1. **There is no UK age rating to output for a general programme.** The correct UK output is
   (a) a scheduling verdict, (b) the earliest permissible broadcast time or window, and
   (c) which Code rules drive it. If a BBFC rating exists for a film, that is an *input*, not an output.
2. **Mixed rule determinacy.** Some rules are bright lines (1.4, 1.14, 1.17, 1.18, 1.23, 1.26) and some
   are explicitly contextual (1.11, 1.16, 1.19, 1.21). A ground-truth test set must score these differently:
   the bright lines have one correct answer; the contextual ones have a defensible range.
3. **"Children" = under 15 but principle = under 18.** An extraction that collapses these will mis-apply
   rules 1.3, 1.10, 1.13 and 1.27.
